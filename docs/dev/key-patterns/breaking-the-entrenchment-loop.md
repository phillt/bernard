# Breaking the entrenchment loop (#372).

- **Breaking the entrenchment loop (#372).** `search()` called `bumpAccess` on **every returned hit**, unconditionally and with no judgment involved — retrieved → score bump → higher retrieval probability → another bump, with nothing ever consulting whether the retrieval helped. Observed on a real store: **31 stale facts with `accessCount` up to 40**, originally due to expire Sept–Oct and renewing indefinitely.
  - **Counting and extending are now separate.** The retrieval is still recorded — it is true, and the count feeds the prune score — but it no longer buys _life_. Endorsement does, and arrives through `recordAccess` with the ids a curator explicitly KEPT after seeing all ~24 candidates in one prompt. An existing test asserted the opposite contract (_"search extends expiresAt when fact is close to expiring"_) and was rewritten, because that contract **was** the loop.
  - **Known consequence, named rather than discovered:** cron and `bernard script` call `search()` and never run `recall-filter`, so a fact used only there gets no TTL extension and expires at 90 days from creation. Re-observation still renews it (#525), and a REPL session's curator still endorses it. The alternative — letting unjudged retrieval renew forever — is the defect.
  - **Three changes to the prune score, and each answers a specific line of #372.** The access term is **capped** at 5 accesses (~2.58) because `log2(accessCount + 1)` was unbounded while `recency` is bounded in (0,1] — by 40 accesses recency was noise. The credit **decays** on a 30-day half-life keyed to `lastAccessed`, because a fact retrieved fifty times in March kept that credit forever; decaying the credit at scoring time rather than halving the stored counter keeps `accessCount` a true count and needs no clock or migration — **and it gives `lastAccessed` its first reader**, a field written since the store was created and read by nothing. And a **newcomer floor** protects a fact for 7 days, because recency alone cannot rescue five new facts at `accessCount` 0 against 31 at up to 40. A floor rather than a bonus, so it cannot stack into a runaway score.
  - **Rejection-as-a-signal is deliberately NOT here**, though the issue body asks for it. A later comment on the issue, carrying #522's research, already moves the answer elsewhere (_"frequency is the wrong axis"_, with a utility proxy replacing rejection), and two further objections settle it: the curator's candidate pool is **deliberately widened** (0.28 against the store's own 0.35, top-8 against top-5, cap 24 against 15), so most rejections are the widening working as designed and a rejection counter would largely measure "was in the widened tail" — a proxy for similarity rank the score already has; and rejection is conditioned on the **query**, not the fact, so dropping a deployment fact on a calendar turn says nothing about it. The Wilson score lower bound goes with it, having no denominator.
  - **The utility proxy the comment proposes is blocked, and the blocker is worth recording**: `REASONING_FAMILIES` (`openai-reasoning`, `xai-grok-reasoning`) never emit `[^Sn]` markers, so a used/retrieved ratio reads **zero for every fact** on those models and would decay the whole store — silently, which is the #520 failure shape. The fail-open guard is the load-bearing part, not the ratio.
  - **The issue's W-TinyLFU suggestion is superseded**, per the same comment: S3-FIFO (SOSP 2023, 6,594 traces) measures W-TinyLFU as _worse than plain FIFO on ~50% of traces at small cache sizes_, which is our regime at 5,000 records. If admission control lands it should take S3-FIFO's shape. Not in this change.
