# Dedup reinforces the survivor instead of discarding the observation (#525).

- **Dedup reinforces the survivor instead of discarding the observation (#525).** On a collision `addFacts` called `continue`: the newcomer was dropped and **the survivor gained nothing** — no counter, no TTL refresh, no `source` update. Re-learning a fact across ten sessions was indistinguishable from learning it once, so repetition — the strongest available evidence that a fact is durable — was invisible to the prune score.
  - **The measurement is what bounds the change, and it contradicts the literature.** Agent-memory work (Mem0, Zep/Graphiti) treats a high-similarity collision as _supersession_, on the grounds that near-duplicate embeddings hide contradictions. Measured against the real provider at `DEDUP_THRESHOLD = 0.92`, **not one contradicting pair reaches the threshold** — the highest is `deploy.sh` vs `release.sh` at 0.8759, and Mumbai/Bangalore is 0.7340. So a collision here really is a near-restatement and reinforcing it is safe; the contradiction problem is real but arrives by a different door, which is #373's, shipped separately.
  - **`observedCount` is a separate counter from `accessCount`, deliberately.** They measure different things: `accessCount` is "was retrieved", which says a fact is topically adjacent to what gets asked; `observedCount` is "was learned again". #372 is about to **cap and decay `accessCount`** specifically to break an entrenchment loop that retrieval creates — folding observations into it would cap and decay them for a reason that has nothing to do with them.
  - **It has a reader, and that is the point.** `source` and `lastAccessed` are both written by this store and read by **nothing**; a third write-only field would record the observation and still fail to save the record from a prune. So it is weighted into the prune score above retrieval (`log2(observed+1) * 2` against `log2(access+1)`), and rendered by `listFacts` so `bernard facts` shows it.
  - **`find`, not `some`.** The survivor has to be in scope to be reinforced, and `some` discards it — which is the shape of the defect. The scan is over the whole unscoped array and **crosses domains**, so a fact can collide with a survivor filed under a different one; that was always true, and reinforcing makes it visible where discarding hid it, which is why `rag:dedup` now logs whether the domains matched.
  - **Reinforcement takes the DEBOUNCED write path** (#533), not the eager one `added` uses: it changes decay metadata, not content. A crash must not lose a fact; losing the note that one was observed again costs a single TTL extension. The turn caches are deliberately untouched — no fact was added or removed, so no search result can have changed.
  - **The extension is monotone, and that guard is load-bearing.** One observation earns ~52 days against the 90-day TTL, so without the guard reinforcing a _fresh_ record would pull its expiry backwards — being learned again would make a fact die sooner. `bumpAccess` carried an identical copy of the guard and had **no test at all**, which is how a mutation aimed at `reinforce` landed there instead and reported a false survivor; both are covered now. The two now share **`extendExpiry`** — only the guard, never the curve, since the curves being different is what makes a re-learned fact outlive an often-retrieved one. A guard right in one copy and dropped in the other fails silently: the record simply expires early, months later, with nothing to trace it to.
